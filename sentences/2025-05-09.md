## Today's Sentences

### 1. Economic Topic Expression
> "When money is tight, families often opt for cheaper, calorie-dense foods."
- **Chinese Meaning**: 当资金紧张时，家庭通常会选择更便宜、高热量的食物。
- **Usage**: Describing how economic pressure affects lifestyle choices
- **Structure**: When + subject + is tight, + result
- **Practice**:
  - When money is tight, people tend to save more.
  - When time is tight, we need to prioritize.
- **Personal Note**: "opt for" is more formal than "choose"

### 2. Trade-off Expression
> "The trade-off between nutrition and affordability."
- **Chinese Meaning**: 营养和价格之间的权衡。
- **Usage**: Discussing difficult choices, weighing pros and cons
- **Structure**: The trade-off between A and B
- **Practice**:
  - The trade-off between work and life balance.
  - The trade-off between quality and quantity.
- **Personal Note**: Very useful in IELTS/TOEFL writing!

### 3. Indicator Expression
> "Body shape has become an unexpected indicator of economic status."
- **Chinese Meaning**: 体型已成为经济状况的一个意外指标。
- **Usage**: Describing how one phenomenon reflects another
- **Structure**: A has become a/an + adjective + indicator of B
- **Practice**:
  - Social media has become a key indicator of popularity.
  - Test scores have become an indicator of academic ability.
- **Personal Note**: "indicator" can be replaced by "marker", "sign", or "signal"

### 4. Correlation Expression
> "There's a strong correlation between socioeconomic status and health outcomes."
- **Chinese Meaning**: 社会经济地位与健康状况之间存在很强的相关性。
- **Usage**: Academic writing, research reports
- **Structure**: There's a correlation between A and B
- **Practice**:
  - There's a correlation between education and income.
  - There's a correlation between exercise and mental health.
- **Personal Note**: ⚠️ Remember: correlation ≠ causation (相关 ≠ 因果)

### 5. Economic Downturn Expression
> "Economic downturns often lead to changes in lifestyle choices."
- **Chinese Meaning**: 经济衰退通常导致生活方式的改变。
- **Usage**: Economic topic discussions
- **Structure**: A often leads to B
- **Practice**:
  - Climate change often leads to extreme weather.
  - Technology advances often lead to job changes.
- **Personal Note**: "lead to" is more diplomatic than "cause"

### 6. Compromise Expression
> "Financial constraints frequently result in compromised dietary choices."
- **Chinese Meaning**: 经济限制经常导致饮食选择的妥协。
- **Usage**: Formal discussions about limitations
- **Structure**: A results in B
- **Practice**:
  - Budget cuts frequently result in reduced services.
  - Lack of sleep frequently results in poor performance.
- **Personal Note**: More formal alternative to "lead to"

## Bonus: Transition Words for Writing

| Function | English | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Showing cause | therefore, thus, hence | Therefore, the situation has improved. |
| Showing effect | as a result, consequently | As a result, sales increased. |
| Adding evidence | moreover, furthermore | Moreover, the data supports this view. |
| Contrasting | however, nevertheless | However, there are limitations. |
