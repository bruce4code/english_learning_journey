## 1. Pronunciation

### repository
- **UK**: /rɪˈpɒz.ɪ.tər.i/
- **US**: /rɪˈpɑː.zə.tɔːr.i/

**Syllables**: re-pos-i-to-ry

**Key sound**: The **s** here is between two vowels → **/z/**

## 2. Etymology (Word Origin)

### Root Analysis

| Component | Language | Meaning |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| **re-** | Latin | again, back |
| **-posit-** | Latin | to place, to put |
| **-ory** | Latin | place of, thing for |

**Full Meaning**:
- **Latin root**: *reponere* (to put back, store away)
  - *re-* = back, again
  - *ponere* = to place, to put
- **Latin suffix**: *-torium* → *-tory* = a place for
- **Literal meaning**: A place where things are stored or deposited

**Historical evolution**:
1. Latin: *repositorium* → a stand or shelf for storing
2. Old French: *repositoire* → repository
3. Middle English: *repositorie* → repository
4. Modern English: *repository* → a place for storing things

**Related words from same root (*ponere* = to put)**:
- **position** (where something is placed)
- **positive** (placed, settled)
- **compose** (com- + pone = put together)
- **impose** (im- + pone = put upon)
- **opponent** (ob- + pone = one who places against)
- **dispose** (dis- + pone = put in order)

## 3. Why does s → /z/? (The Vowel + s + Vowel Rule)

### The Rule

> When a letter **s** appears between two vowels, it is often voiced to **/z/**

**Formula**: `Vowel + s + Vowel` → **/z/**

**Why?** 
In English, unvoiced /s/ between vowels naturally becomes voiced /z/ because the vocal cords continue vibrating from the surrounding vowel sounds, making it easier to pronounce.

---

### Repository Breakdown

```
re  -  p o   -  s  i  -  to  -  ry
       vowel   s   vowel 
                ↓
               /z/
```

The **s** in **repository** is between vowel sounds:
- Before: the 'o' in *po* (vowel)
- After: the 'i' (vowel)
- **Result**: s → /z/

## 5. Exceptions to the Rule

Not all s between vowels becomes /z/. Some remain /s/:

| Word | Pronunciation | Notes |
|------|---------------|-------|
| **basis** | /ˈbeɪsɪs/ | Greek origin, retains /s/ |
| **crisis** | /ˈkraɪsɪs/ | Greek origin |
| **thesis** | /ˈθiːsɪs/ | Greek origin |
| **focus** | /ˈfəʊkəs/ | Single s, not between two pronounced vowels |
| **sassy** | /ˈsæsi/ | Double s keeps /s/ sound |

**Rule of thumb**: When the word has a Latin root, s between vowels tends to be /z/. Words of Greek origin often keep the /s/ sound.
